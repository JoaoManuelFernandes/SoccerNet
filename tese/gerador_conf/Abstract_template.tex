{\Large
Jersey Recognition and Team Affiliation on Soccer Game State Reconstruction}

\mdseries
\end{flushleft}
\normalsize
\let\clearpage\relax
\chapter{ABSTRACT}
\endgroup

\noindent The SoccerNet Game State Reconstruction (GSR) challenge asks for the position of every
player on a map of the pitch, together with their team, role and jersey number, from a video captured
by a single camera. In the reference solution, reading the jersey number and assigning each player to their team and
to the side of the pitch they defend lag behind the other tasks. The jersey number reader was replaced, the team and pitch-side modules
were rewritten, and a kit colour descriptor was added to re-identification. All other modules were left
unchanged, so that any gain can be attributed to the changes made.

The number is now read by a multimodal vision-and-language model, applied only to the player's back,
cropped by a pose model. Only the readings in which the model itself reports being
confident are counted, a rule that cut wrong numbers to about a third. For team assignment, the
appearance-based clustering now checks its own quality and falls back on kit colour when
re-identification does not separate the teams. The pitch side is decided by a sequence of criteria
that ends with the goalkeeper.

On the $159$ annotated videos of SoccerNet-GSR, against the reference solution under the same conditions, jersey number accuracy rose from @@JNR_ALL_O@@ to @@JNR_ALL_N@@, and team and pitch-side
accuracy from @@TEAM_ALL_O@@ to @@TEAM_ALL_N@@. GS-HOTA, the official challenge metric, went from
@@GS_TEST_O@@ to @@GS_TEST_N@@ on the test set, with gains of the same order on validation and on the
training set, which was not used during development. The whole gain comes from the two tasks that were
changed. The remaining error comes mostly from numbers that are illegible in the image and, for teams,
from the role classifier, which was not changed.
